# Conversational context

Use this guide for emails, team messages, chat updates, short meeting summaries, comments, and lightweight coordination text.

## Goal

Help the reader act quickly without making the text cold. Put the point, ask, status, or decision near the top.

## Use this shape

1. Start with the reason for the message.
2. Give the minimum context needed to act.
3. State the ask, owner, decision, deadline, or next step.
4. Close only if a close adds something.

## Do

- Use short paragraphs.
- Use direct questions when the message needs a reply.
- Keep greetings and sign-offs proportional to the relationship.
- Name blockers, dates, and owners.
- Use natural warmth where it belongs.
- Let small imperfections stand if they make the voice human and clear.

## Avoid

- Generic openings like "I hope you are well" unless that is truly the writer's style.
- Praise-padding before the point.
- Long background before the ask.
- Formal nouns where a verb is clearer.
- Ending with "let me know if you have any questions" by default.

## Patterns

Bad: "I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out regarding the upcoming migration and share a few important considerations."

Good: "The migration is ready for Thursday. We still need one approval from Risk before we can merge."

Bad: "It would be greatly appreciated if you could provide your feedback at your earliest convenience."

Good: "Can you review the draft before 14:00?"

Bad: "The meeting covered several important topics and resulted in valuable alignment across stakeholders."

Good: "We agreed to keep the old endpoint until 1 September. Maria checks whether any batch jobs still call it."
